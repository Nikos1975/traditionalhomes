The coast around Elounda is not one continuous beach. Schisma, Chiona, Kolokytha and Plaka are close enough to belong to the same day out, but each asks for a different expectation: a central organised beach, a smaller shore beside the salt pans, an unorganised cove, or a public village waterfront. The distinctions are more useful than a ranking. For the wider geography behind them, begin with [Elounda and Mirabello Bay](/en/blog/elounda-and-mirabello-bay/).

## Schisma: central Elounda

Schisma is the central organised beach area of Elounda. It is the clearest choice when the aim is to swim close to the centre rather than make an outing of the approach itself. The municipal visitor information places it at the edge of the bay, while the 2026 Blue Flag list names Schisma as a separate sector.

That is enough to establish its role in this guide. The article does not list current sunbeds, showers, lifeguard cover or accessibility equipment: those arrangements can change and should be checked locally. From Mavrikiano, the [location guide](/en/location/) and the [Mavrikiano guide](/en/guide/mavrikiano/) provide the wider village context.

## Chiona and the salt-pan shoreline

Chiona is a separate beach beside the Elounda salt pans. It is better understood as part of the lower shoreline landscape beyond the centre than as another name for Schisma. The salt pans, canal and windmills add the historical context; [Elounda Salt Pans and the Poros Windmills](/en/blog/elounda-salt-pans-and-poros-windmills/) explains that setting in more detail.

The practical point is simply to keep names clear. The local Poros channel and causeway should not be confused with the separately named Poros resort-coast sectors that appear in official monitoring and award material.

## Kolokytha / Agios Loukas

Kolokytha is an unorganised beach in a small bay, with a downhill path forming the final part of the approach. Its access is the main limitation. Owner-supplied local observations from 3 August 2026 describe the road as rocky, rough and mostly single-lane, with occasional passing places. The parking area is an informal, uneven open area in the natural landscape rather than a surfaced or organised car park.

A higher-clearance vehicle is more suitable, and the route is not recommended for inexperienced drivers. This does not mean that an SUV is mandatory or that ordinary cars categorically cannot reach the beach. Conditions change, so it is sensible to check locally before setting out. This guide deliberately gives no fixed walking distance, parking-capacity estimate, boat timetable or current-facility list.

## Main Plaka beach

The main Plaka beach is public and organised. Its character is different from central Elounda: it is a village shoreline on the coast facing Spinalonga, and it should not be merged with the adjacent hotel-operated sections simply because the waterline continues.

## Phaea Blue beach sector

Phaea Blue directly adjoins the beach in its sector. The hotel operates the sunbeds and related facilities there, and the 2026 Blue Flag record names Phāea Blue as the programme manager for the Plaka/Phaea Blue sector.

The shoreline can also be approached independently from the church side, so it should not be described as exclusively private. At the same time, that approach does not establish whether non-guests may use hotel facilities. The article makes no claim about the hotel’s current policy.

## Southern resort beaches: use the names carefully

The 2026 Blue Flag list for the Municipality of Agios Nikolaos includes several hotel-linked Elounda sectors, including Driros/Domes of Elounda, Pigaidakia Eloundas/Elounda Village, Poros 1/Elounda Bay Palace and Poros 2/Elounda Beach. These are useful official names, but they should not be inflated into a list of separate public beaches.

The same caution applies to the Greek Bathing Water Profiles Registry. Monitoring and award records can identify named sectors and sampling places. They do not, by themselves, confirm current parking, lifeguards, facility provision, shoreline boundaries or the conditions under which a hotel operates its beach facilities.

There are smaller coves around the peninsula, but this is not a driving guide to them. ## Reading the coast in practical terms

For a central organised beach, Schisma is the reference point. Chiona belongs to the salt-pan side of the coast. Kolokytha is the unorganised option, where the approach is part of the decision. Main Plaka is public and organised, while Phaea Blue is a neighbouring hotel-operated sector with its own facilities and a separate policy question.

That framework keeps the coastline legible without assuming that every named beach, award label or hotel frontage describes the same kind of place. For a stay in the villages above and beyond the waterfront, see the [houses in Mavrikiano and Vrouchas](/en/houses/).
