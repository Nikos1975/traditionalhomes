The coast around Elounda is not one continuous beach. Schisma, Chiona, Kolokytha, Driros and Plaka are close enough to belong to the same day out, but each asks for a different expectation: a central organised beach, a smaller shore beside the salt pans, an unorganised cove, a hotel-adjacent sector or a village waterfront. The distinctions are more useful than a ranking. For the wider geography behind them, begin with [Elounda and Mirabello Bay](/en/blog/elounda-and-mirabello-bay/).

## Are any Elounda beaches private?

No beach in this guide should be described as privately owned in the ordinary sense. Under the Greek framework, the seashore and beach are common-use areas, and a simple-use concession does not turn the shoreline into private property. Where a concession applies, free, unobstructed and safe public passage must be preserved.

Hotels and other businesses may manage sunbeds, umbrellas, towels, bars and other services on defined sections. Those facilities may be reserved, paid or subject to the operator's policy. This is a practical distinction about the shoreline, not a right to enter a hotel building, internal hotel grounds, car park or other private property in order to reach it.

## Schisma: central Elounda

Schisma is the central organised beach area of Elounda. It is the clearest choice when the aim is to swim close to the centre rather than make an outing of the approach itself. The municipal visitor information places it at the edge of the bay, while the 2026 Blue Flag list names Schisma as a separate sector.

Seasonal facilities and lifeguard arrangements can change, so check locally when these services are important to your visit. From Mavrikiano, the [location guide](/en/location/) and the [Mavrikiano guide](/en/guide/mavrikiano/) provide the wider village context.

## Chiona and the salt-pan shoreline

Chiona is a separate beach beside the Elounda salt pans. It is better understood as part of the lower shoreline landscape beyond the centre than as another name for Schisma. The salt pans, canal and windmills add the historical context; [Elounda Salt Pans and the Poros Windmills](/en/blog/elounda-salt-pans-and-poros-windmills/) explains that setting in more detail.

The practical point is simply to keep names clear. The local Poros channel and causeway should not be confused with the separately named Poros resort-coast sectors that appear in official monitoring and award material.

## Kolokytha / Agios Loukas

Kolokytha is an unorganised beach in a small bay, with a downhill path forming the final part of the approach. The approach road is rocky, rough and mostly single-lane, with occasional passing places. The parking area is an informal, uneven open area in the natural landscape rather than a surfaced or organised car park.

## Main Plaka beach

The main Plaka beach is public and organised. Its character is different from central Elounda: it is a village shoreline on the coast facing Spinalonga. The public village beach is separate from the hotel-managed beach sections farther along the shoreline.

## Driros / Domes of Elounda

Driros is the officially named Driros/Domes of Elounda beach sector on the Elounda–Plaka coast. The 2026 Blue Flag record names Domes of Elounda as the sector's programme manager, and the resort directly adjoins Driros beach.

Before the present resort development, Driros was known locally as a small beach of fine, pale-beige sand beside the former Hotel Driros. In a local recollection supplied by Elounda Traditional Homes, Nikos Pasparakis remembers families from Elounda swimming there and visiting the hotel's seaside taverna. The hotel later changed ownership. This is historical context only, not evidence of current access, sand conditions or facility policy.

The beach itself is not legally private: the shoreline remains a common-use area under the Greek framework. Domes of Elounda manages its sunbeds, umbrellas, towels and other beach services, however, and use of those facilities is subject to the resort's policy. Check practical shoreline routes and current facility conditions locally; do not assume use of resort parking, an entrance through the resort or access to its internal grounds.

## Phaea Blue beach sector

Phaea Blue directly adjoins the beach in its sector. The shoreline remains publicly accessible as a common-use area under the Greek framework, while the hotel operates the sunbeds and related facilities there. The 2026 Blue Flag record names Phāea Blue as the programme manager for the Plaka/Phaea Blue sector.

The shoreline can also be approached independently from the church side. The facilities remain hotel-managed, and non-guests should not assume that they may use them. This does not establish a right to pass through the hotel's private premises, car park or internal grounds; check practical route conditions locally.

## Southern resort beaches: use the names carefully

The 2026 Blue Flag list for the Municipality of Agios Nikolaos includes several hotel-linked Elounda sectors, including Driros/Domes of Elounda, Pigaidakia Eloundas/Elounda Village, Poros 1/Elounda Bay Palace and Poros 2/Elounda Beach. These official names identify monitored or managed beach sectors, but they do not establish the practical access route, parking arrangements or rights to use hotel-managed facilities.

The same caution applies to the Greek Bathing Water Profiles Registry. Monitoring and award records can identify named sectors and sampling places. They do not, by themselves, confirm current parking, lifeguards, facility provision, shoreline boundaries or the conditions under which a hotel operates its beach facilities.

## Minor coves on the peninsula

There are smaller coves around the peninsula, but they are not covered here as driving destinations. Access and conditions can change, so use current local information before setting out.

## Reading the coast in practical terms

For a central organised beach, Schisma is the reference point. Chiona belongs to the salt-pan side of the coast. Kolokytha is the unorganised option, where the approach is part of the decision. Driros and Phaea Blue are hotel-adjacent sectors: their shorelines remain common-use areas, while their equipment and services are managed by the adjoining hotels. Main Plaka is the public, organised village section.

That framework keeps the coastline legible without assuming that every named beach, award label or hotel frontage describes the same kind of place. For a stay in the villages above and beyond the waterfront, see the [houses in Mavrikiano and Vrouchas](/en/houses/).
