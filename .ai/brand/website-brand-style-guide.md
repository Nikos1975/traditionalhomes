# Website Brand Style Guide
## Elounda Traditional Homes of Crete

## Brand Position

Elounda Traditional Homes of Crete is a quiet collection of restored traditional stone houses in Mavrikiano and one villa in Vrouchas, above Elounda.

The website should present the houses as real places with architectural character, village context, and practical access to the Elounda area.

The goal is not to sell a holiday through emotion.
The goal is to describe each property clearly, calmly, and credibly.

## Brand Character

The brand should feel:

- calm
- specific
- rooted in place
- understated
- trustworthy
- architecturally aware
- practical without sounding transactional

The brand should not feel:

- Airbnb-like
- sales-led
- generic luxury travel
- breathless
- sentimental
- overly intimate
- promotional
- resort-like

## Core Voice

Write in a restrained editorial tone.

The voice should be:

- clear
- composed
- descriptive
- observational
- useful
- modestly elegant
- grounded in physical reality

The voice should not be:

- gushy
- theatrical
- poetic for its own sake
- overconfident
- persuasive in a sales sense
- inflated with adjectives

## What the Website Should Sound Like

The website should sound like:

- a serious property collection
- a local editorial guide with architectural awareness
- a calm host who respects the reader’s intelligence
- a place-based brand with clarity and restraint

The website should not sound like:

- an Airbnb listing
- a hotel chain
- a travel influencer
- a tourism board brochure
- a luxury lifestyle ad
- a booking funnel

## Writing Priorities

Always prioritize:

1. physical truth
2. layout clarity
3. practical suitability
4. architectural character
5. place and setting
6. calm specificity
7. useful distinctions
8. concise, readable structure

## Copy Principles

1. Describe the house, do not sell the stay.
2. State what is physically true.
3. Keep the copy specific to the property.
4. Use layout to reduce misunderstanding.
5. Use suitability notes to build trust.
6. Separate atmosphere from exaggeration.
7. Prefer precision over flourish.
8. If facts are limited, write less.
9. Do not repeat destination copy inside every section.
10. Avoid any sentence that could belong to a generic vacation rental listing.

## Language to Use

Prefer language like:

- traditional Cretan stone home
- hillside village of Mavrikiano
- quieter village setting above Elounda
- preserved architectural character
- multi-level layout shaped by the character of older homes
- sea-view veranda
- private courtyard
- private balcony with sea view
- ground-floor house with shared pool access
- outdoor kitchenette and barbecue on the veranda
- free private parking is available nearby
- well suited to guests comfortable with stairs
- calm base close to Elounda
- village setting with practical access to the waterfront

## Language to Avoid

Avoid:

- escape
- unforgettable memories
- magical
- dream stay
- paradise
- hidden gem
- soul-filled
- reconnect with yourself
- perfect for
- ultimate
- breathtaking, unless something is literally the central visual fact
- dates fill fast
- book now
- do not miss it
- once-in-a-lifetime
- romantic retreat
- luxury oasis
- tranquil haven
- authentic experience

## Bad Examples

Do not write things like:

- “a soul-filled Cretan escape”
- “where unforgettable memories are made”
- “perfect for couples, families, and cultural travelers”
- “book your escape today”
- “the ultimate escape”
- “slow down, breathe deeply, and reconnect with the essence of Crete”
- “wake up inspired”
- “romantic sea-view paradise”
- “magical Cretan stay”

## Better Examples

Prefer sentences like:

- “House Argyro is a traditional Cretan stone home in Mavrikiano, above Elounda Bay.”
- “The sea-view veranda is the main outdoor living space.”
- “The house unfolds across several levels in the traditional way of older Cretan homes.”
- “Free private parking is available nearby.”
- “This house is well suited to guests comfortable with a multi-level layout.”
- “A separate private courtyard provides a second, more sheltered outdoor space.”
- “The setting offers a quieter village atmosphere while keeping Elounda close at hand.”

## Tone by Page Type

### Homepage

The homepage should position the collection clearly.

It should explain:

- what the collection is
- where it is
- what kind of setting it offers
- why the houses are distinct

It should not rely on mood-writing or emotional travel language.

### House Pages

House pages should be decision pages.

They should help the reader understand:

- what the house is
- how it works spatially
- what the main outdoor space is
- what the location means in practice
- whether the house suits their needs

### Location Pages

Location pages should be informative and place-based.

They should explain:

- practical geography
- village context
- nearby routes, beaches, and landmarks
- why the setting differs from staying directly in the center

They should not sound like tourism copy.

### Blog Posts

Blog posts should feel like calm local editorial.

They should be:

- informed
- place-based
- practical
- historically aware when relevant
- free from travel clichés

## Suitability Language

When describing fit, use:

- well suited to
- particularly suited to
- best for guests comfortable with
- works well for

Do not use:

- perfect for
- ideal for everyone
- made for
- designed for unforgettable stays

## Practical Honesty

Never smooth over friction.

If a house has:

- internal stairs
- split levels
- shared pool arrangements
- step-free limitations
- access constraints
- unusual kitchen placement
- balcony or veranda supervision issues

state them clearly and calmly.

Clarity improves trust.

## Architectural and Spatial Language

Prefer words like:

- stone
- veranda
- courtyard
- balcony
- arch
- level
- upper floor
- entrance level
- living area
- dining nook
- handmade furniture
- traditional woodwork
- preserved details
- natural materials
- older home
- village lane
- hillside setting

These are better than abstract emotional phrases.

## Emotional Register

Keep emotion low and controlled.

It is acceptable to suggest atmosphere through real detail.
It is not acceptable to promise feelings.

Good:
- “The upper bedroom opens directly to the sea-view veranda.”
- “The elevated setting gives the house a quieter atmosphere.”

Not good:
- “You will fall in love with the view.”
- “This house will create unforgettable memories.”
- “You will reconnect with your soul here.”

## Sentence Check

Before finalizing any sentence, check:

- Is this physically true?
- Is this specific to this house or place?
- Is this useful to a serious guest?
- Is this calmer than Airbnb?
- Is this saying something concrete rather than creating mood?

If the answer is no, rewrite or remove it.

## Final Rule

The website should describe the houses and their setting with calm confidence.

It should feel considered, local, and durable.

It should never sound like a listing trying to persuade the reader to book immediately.
