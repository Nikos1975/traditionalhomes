# Elounda on the Imperial Airways Route: verification dossier

**Working title:** *Elounda on the Imperial Airways Route: Flying Boats, Pilots and the Scipio Accident*

**Slug:** `elounda-imperial-airways-flying-boats`

**Scope:** second-stage reconciliation; this is not an article draft.
**Research cut-off:** 2026-08-05.

## 1. Executive reconciliation summary

The evidence securely establishes Mirabella/Mirabello Bay as an Imperial Airways flying-boat base by May 1930. Webb Miller, travelling on the weekly India service, recorded a landing beside Spinalonga, an overnight aboard the 100-ton *Imperia*, and a dawn departure for Alexandria. His account names the pilot as **Jimmy Youell**. The proposed expansion to A. B. H. (Alan Bruce Hamilton) Youell remains a strong research lead, not an official crew assignment.

The strongest contemporary/company evidence supports *Imperia* as accommodation for pilots and passengers, and as a wireless and refuelling point. It does **not** yet prove the mechanics of fuel transfer, a complete service chronology, or the asserted Knikker pedigree.

The Scipio accident is securely dated to 22 August 1936. Hansard confirms two deaths, that the inspector attributed one reason to a misjudged approach which led the pilot to open the engines, and that there was no evidence that an engine failed to respond. Detailed narratives concerning a downdraught, tailplane setting, wing separation and rescue are presently only specialist/philatelic or later reporting; they must not be presented as official findings until the full report is obtained.

**Decision:** not article-ready. A publication-safe short note could use Miller and Hansard alone, but the requested 1,800–2,500-word pilot-led microhistory requires the accident file, original timetables, and crew/ship records.

## 4. Authoritative operational role of Mirabello

Miller’s contemporaneous narrative establishes a functioning air base at Mirabella in May 1930, including an overnight. Lambert & Butler publicity, as reproduced in later reporting, calls *Imperia* a wireless station and refuelling point. The safe formulation is: **“Mirabella was an Imperial Airways flying-boat operational base and refuelling point, with overnight accommodation when a weather decision deferred the Alexandria crossing.”** Do not call every Athens–Alexandria service a scheduled passenger stop.

## 6. Corrected chronology

- May 1930: Miller’s through journey documents Mirabella, *Imperia*, a pilot named Jimmy Youell and a delayed departure for Alexandria.
- 22 August 1936: Short S.17 Kent *Scipio*, G-ABFA, crashes at Mirabella; two deaths.
- 18 November 1936: Commons question and answer, HC Deb vol. 317 c1778.
- 28 December 1936: Short S.8 Calcutta *City of Alexandria*, G-EBVG, is overturned/destroyed in a storm while stopped at Mirabella; no crew aboard according to the Egypt Study Circle account.
- 1939: Imperial Airways merges into BOAC. This is corporate context, not evidence of the end date of Mirabella use.

## 10. Detailed verified pilot profiles

**Jimmy Youell (full identity unresolved).** Miller describes him as “Jimmy Youell, star pilot for Imperial Airways”; the account begins in May 1930 and follows him from the European landplane leg to the three-engined flying boat. A contemporary 1934 report gives A. B. H. Youell’s initials and records 8,394 flying hours, making the proposed expansion plausible. Neither item is an Imperial Airways movement log or personnel record tying A. B. H. Youell to Miller’s flight, so no full biography should enter an article yet.

**A. S. Wilcockson.** The accessed sources establish the initials and Imperial Airways career, but not a full name or a roster-level proof of his exact *Scipio* role. Do not publish a profile until the accident report or staff roster is consulted.

## 11. Webb Miller and serum-flight investigation

**Citation used:** Webb Miller, *I Found No Peace* (New York: Simon and Schuster, 1936; online scan has 358 pages), chapter XV, “Magic Carpet”, printed pp. 175–89; relevant pp. 177–81. Accessed 2026-08-05.

The journey starts in May 1930. At pp. 177–80 Miller identifies “Jimmy Youell,” records Phaleron refuelling from a motorboat, and gives the 220-mile hop to Mirabella. At p. 181 he records the weather decision, overnight aboard *Imperia*, 4 a.m. call, dawn take-off and Alexandria destination. He says a motorboat carried serum onward to Cairo, where a special train was waiting. The memoir identification is therefore **Jimmy Youell**; the expansion to A. B. H. Youell remains unconfirmed by an airline record. The aircraft remains unidentified.

Miller’s 370-mile and “longest regular oversea flight in Europe” wording is attributable memoir, not independently verified comparative fact.

## 12. Scipio official-report search results

**Found:** the official parliamentary summary: “Imperial Airways Liner ‘Scipio’ (Accident),” HC Deb 18 November 1936, vol. 317, col. 1778, question 32, Mr Perkins; answer Sir Philip Sassoon.

**Not found:** full inspector’s report, inspector name, report title/date/page count, recommendations or catalogue reference. Hansard is a short official summary, not the full investigation. Required searches: The National Archives AIR accident-investigation series and British Airways Heritage Collection catalogue, using G-ABFA, *Scipio*, Mirabella/Mirabello and 22 August 1936.

## 13. Scipio accident reconstruction

**Established sequence:** *Scipio* crashed at Mirabella on 22 August 1936; two people died; it was on the Alexandria-to-Europe route. The crash-mail record says four crew and five of seven passengers survived.

**Official causal finding available:** the inspector reported that one reason was that the pilot misjudged the approach and consequently opened the engines. No engine non-response was evidenced.

**Contemporary/specialist reporting:** rough sea, lateral wind, exact impact location, tailplane setting, rapid sinking, 38 bags and specific rescue movements occur in later specialist crash-mail accounts. Treat as source-attributed, not official.

**Unresolved:** physical sequence, full causal analysis, occupant roles and recommendations.

## 16. Rescue and salvage reconstruction

Nordic Air Crash Mail says *Imperia* arrived promptly, cared for occupants, HMS *Durban* later took people and some mail to Athens (25 August), and all 38 bags were recovered within six hours. A Greek local-history post reproduces/ translates a Reuters-derived account mentioning local authorities and doctors. These are useful leads, but neither has been checked against the original Reuters dispatch, naval log or Greek records. Publication-safe wording: **“Specialist crash-mail accounts record an *Imperia* and HMS *Durban* response; the underlying official and naval records still need verification.”**

## 17. City of Alexandria incident assessment

The Egypt Study Circle records G-EBVG at Mirabella on 28 December 1936, apparently returning light, damaged in a storm and overturned by another gust next day; no crew were aboard. That confirms continued use after *Scipio*, but is specialist secondary evidence. It belongs in the final article as a short, carefully attributed afterword only after an original contemporary report is found.

## 18. Mail and cargo evidence

The *Scipio* carried mail; water-damaged crash covers and crash-mail studies corroborate it. Miller directly witnessed three serum boxes carried for a girl in Cairo. Do not state that Mirabella specialised in medical cargo or that exact mail recovery was official until postal records are consulted.
