# Proposed Article Wording — Research Stage Only

This wording is approved only as a research recommendation. It is not an article draft and must be rechecked against the claim register before publication.

## Scope opener

“Elounda’s swimming places are easier to understand as a sequence of different shores than as a single beach. The central municipal shore at Schisma, the low ground around Poros, the natural cove at Kolokytha and the public beach at Plaka each involve a different kind of approach and different expectations.”

## Kolokytha / Agios Loukas

“Kolokytha is an unorganised natural beach. The approach road is rocky, rough and mostly single-lane, with occasional places for vehicles to pass. Parking is an informal, uneven open area in the natural landscape rather than a surfaced or organised car park. A higher-clearance vehicle is more suitable, and the route is not recommended for inexperienced drivers. This is not a claim that an SUV is mandatory or that ordinary cars cannot reach the beach; conditions should be checked locally before setting out.”

Do not add a walking distance, a claim about present parking rules, boat-arrival times, crowd levels, beach services or an assertion that the road is suitable for every vehicle.

## Plaka

“The main Plaka beach is a public, organised shoreline. It should be considered separately from the adjacent hotel-operated beach facilities, even where the coast appears continuous.”

Do not describe the public Plaka shoreline as a hotel beach, and do not use the hotel-sector facilities as evidence of what is available on the whole village shore.

## Phaea Blue sector

“Phaea Blue directly adjoins the beach, and the sunbeds and associated facilities in its sector are operated by the hotel. The shoreline can also be approached independently from the church side, so it should not be described as exclusively private.”

“The article does not state whether non-guests may use the hotel’s facilities. That depends on the hotel’s current policy and needs direct confirmation.”

## Monitoring and award language

“Official bathing-water and Blue Flag names help identify monitored or awarded sectors, but they do not by themselves settle beach boundaries, public access or use of hotel facilities.”

## Wording rejected for this article

- “An SUV is required for Kolokytha.”
- “Ordinary cars cannot reach Kolokytha.”
- “Kolokytha has an organised car park.”
- “Plaka beach is private.”
- “Phaea Blue owns the whole shoreline.”
- “Non-guests can use Phaea Blue’s sunbeds or facilities.”
