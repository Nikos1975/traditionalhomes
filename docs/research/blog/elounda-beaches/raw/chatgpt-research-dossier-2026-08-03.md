# Elounda Beaches and Recognised Swimming Places: Research Dossier

**Research date:** 3 August 2026  
**Geographic focus:** Schisma Elounda, Mavrikiano, the salt pans and Poros channel, the Kolokytha peninsula, the Elounda–Plaka shoreline, Plaka and the southern resort coast  
**Purpose:** Evidence base for a future Traditional Homes article; this is not the website article  
**Editorial brief:** The attached brief requires a geographically accurate distinction between formal beaches, natural coves, rocky bathing places, commercially named resort beaches and locations incorrectly or approximately marketed as “Elounda.” fileciteturn0file0

## Executive findings

The most defensible editorial meaning of **“Elounda beaches”** is not every beach promoted by hotels or travel websites within driving distance of Elounda. It is the sequence of bathing places from the southern Elounda resort coast, through Schisma and the salt-pan shoreline, around the Kolokytha peninsula, and north along the road to Plaka. All of these are within the Municipality of Agios Nikolaos, but they do not all belong to the main Elounda settlement, and several are hotel-associated beaches whose practical public access is unclear. Plaka is part of the Vrouchas community rather than the central Elounda settlement, although it is geographically and functionally part of the wider Elounda lagoon area. citeturn15view1turn16search0turn16search1turn24search8turn24search20

The research identified **thirteen defensible beach or swimming-place entries**, grouped into four practical types:

| Type | Defensible entries |
|---|---|
| Settlement and municipal beaches | Schisma/Central Elounda Beach; Chiona |
| Informal or natural swimming places | Poros channel edge; Kolokytha; Vathi/Armos; northern Kolokytha coves; Tsifliki roadside coves |
| Plaka-area beaches | Plaka village shoreline; Plaka/Phaea Blue sector; Driros |
| Southern resort-coast beaches | Poros; Poros North; Pigadakia Elounda |

A further group of commercial names—Porto Elounda, Elounda Mare, Elounda Blu, Elounda Beach and Elounda Bay Palace—should be treated as **operator labels or sub-sectors**, not automatically as separate geographic beaches. The Municipality’s 2026 Blue Flag list uses several of these commercial labels, while the national bathing-water registry uses broader names such as Poros, Poros North and Pigadakia Elounda. citeturn15view0turn16search1turn19search0turn20search2

The principal naming problems are:

1. **“Elounda Main Beach,” “Elounda Municipal Beach” and “Schisma Beach” are often presented as different beaches.** Official monitoring and municipal use support treating Schisma as the principal central municipal beach or, at minimum, as an overlapping shoreline. A separate authoritative profile for an independently bounded “Elounda Main Beach” was not found. citeturn16search0turn14search10turn24search3turn24search5
2. **“Poros” has two meanings.** Locally it denotes the channel and causeway near the salt pans; in the official bathing-water registry, Poros and Poros North are hotel-dominated bays approximately three kilometres south of Elounda. These must not be conflated. citeturn19search0turn20search2turn24search11
3. **“Kolokytha” can refer to an island, a peninsula-side beach, a bay or the wider peninsula landscape.** The commonly visited beach is the small sandy cove near Agios Loukas on the eastern side of the peninsula, facing the separate Kolokytha islet. citeturn21search5turn21search17turn24search13
4. **Plaka is commonly called an Elounda beach even though it is a separate village associated administratively with Vrouchas.** “Near Elounda” or “in the wider Elounda area” is accurate; “in central Elounda” is not. citeturn15view1turn24search20
5. **Search results routinely mix beaches within Elounda with beaches nearer Agios Nikolaos.** Almyros, Ammoudi, Kitroplatia and Havania regularly appear in “Elounda beaches” results despite being outside the strict Elounda coast. citeturn24search2turn24search4turn24search16turn24search17

The strongest practical distinction is not “best versus worst.” It is:

- **Schisma** for straightforward, central, car-free or family-oriented bathing.
- **Chiona** for a quieter salt-pan-side swim with a rockier entry.
- **Kolokytha** for a natural cove requiring an unpaved approach and a walk, without dependable facilities.
- **Plaka** for pebble bathing, roadside access, tavernas and Spinalonga views.
- **The southern resort bays** for highly modified, sheltered and serviced bathing, but with unresolved non-guest access arrangements.
- **Vathi, Tsifliki and the northern peninsula coves** for more self-sufficient swimmers and short exploratory visits rather than conventional beach days. citeturn19search0turn21search5turn22search13turn24search9turn24search11

The highest-risk claims for publication concern current parking, lifeguards, accessible entrances, sunbeds, hotel access, boat-excursion timing and crowd levels. The national beach profiles describing Poros and Pigadakia were issued in May 2015; they remain valuable for physical geography but should not be treated as proof of 2026 operational arrangements. citeturn19search0turn20search2

## Geographic definition and verified inventory

**Recommended geographic boundary**

For the future article, “Elounda beaches” should mean:

> Publicly recognised bathing places from Pigadakia and the Poros resort bays in the south, through Schisma, Chiona and the Poros causeway, around the accessible parts of the Kolokytha peninsula, and north along the coast to Plaka and Driros.

This is a **practical visitor area**, not a claim that every location lies within one settlement or community. Plaka should be labelled “near Elounda, within the wider lagoon area.” Pigadakia and the official Poros beaches should be labelled “on the southern Elounda resort coast.” citeturn15view1turn19search0turn20search2turn24search8turn24search20

Beaches around Agios Nikolaos—including Havania, Ammoudi, Kitroplatia, Ammoudara and Almyros—should not form part of the core inventory. They may appear in a short “farther afield” box, clearly labelled as alternatives outside Elounda. citeturn24search2turn24search16turn24search17

## Detailed profiles and boundary clarifications

**Schisma and the central municipal beach**

Schisma is the principal defensible central beach name. It is an official bathing-water monitoring identity, appeared in the Municipality’s 2026 Blue Flag list, and is used for current municipal events at the central Elounda beach. A map source places it at approximately 35.26135, 25.72342. citeturn16search0turn15view0turn24search3

Commercial guides frequently describe “Elounda Main Beach,” “Elounda Municipal Beach” and “Skisma” separately. That distinction is not supported by a correspondingly clear set of official monitoring profiles. The safest publication wording is:

> “The main organised beach in central Elounda is generally identified officially as Schisma; maps and visitor guides also call it Elounda Municipal Beach or Elounda Main Beach.”

Avoid publishing a list that treats Schisma and “Elounda Main Beach” as two fully verified, separate beaches unless a field survey identifies distinct boundaries and signage. citeturn16search0turn24search0turn24search5

The central location, paved approach, settlement services and current bus frequency make Schisma the most practical choice for visitors without a car. Current KTEL schedules valid from 1 July 2026 listed frequent daily services between Agios Nikolaos and Elounda. Timetables are seasonal and should be linked rather than reproduced permanently in the final article. citeturn22search2turn22search13

**Chiona**

Chiona is a separate, officially monitored and Blue Flag-listed bathing place immediately east of Schisma, beside the old salt pans. A local geographic explanation derives its name from the former salt warehouse and identifies a surviving stone storage building dating from 1830. The shore is described as mixed sand and pebble with a shallow but rocky seabed, including sharp stones and seaweed. citeturn16search0turn15view0turn24search11

Chiona is a better candidate for a quiet swim or snorkelling than for presenting as an uncomplicated children’s beach. The water may be shallow, but the entry conditions are less forgiving than at a conventional sandy municipal beach. Any statement that it is “perfect for young children” would ignore the documented rock and sharp-stone conditions. citeturn24search11

**Poros channel and the salt-pan shoreline**

The local Poros channel is the narrow cut beneath the bridge connecting the mainland shoreline with the peninsula route. The area is culturally significant because the canal, salt pans and shoreline windmills explain how the lagoon and working coast developed. Current Traditional Homes content attributes the salt-pan and windmill account to the Ephorate of Antiquities of Lasithi. citeturn23search3

This shoreline can be included as a **recognised informal bathing area**, but it should not be described as a normal beach. There are rocks, shallow modified edges, archaeological remains in the wider Olous area and boat passage through the channel. It is also essential to state that this Poros is not the same as the national registry’s Poros beach south of Elounda. citeturn19search0turn24search11

**Olous and the salt pans**

The submerged Olous shoreline is useful context for understanding why parts of the central coast are shallow, modified and historically sensitive. It should not be positioned as a beach attraction or as a place for unrestricted exploration of archaeological remains. The future article should limit history to a concise orientation paragraph and link to the existing salt-pans and Elounda-history articles. citeturn23search2turn23search3turn23search4

**Kolokytha clarification**

The name is used at four geographic levels:

| Term | Defensible meaning |
|---|---|
| Kolokytha peninsula | The landform east and north of the Poros channel, also frequently called the Spinalonga or Kalydon peninsula |
| Kolokytha islet | The small offshore island facing the principal beach |
| Kolokytha Beach | The principal sandy cove on the peninsula’s eastern side near Agios Loukas |
| Kolokytha/Agios Loukas bay | The water and shoreline around that cove, sometimes used broadly enough to include adjacent boat-swim stops |

The principal beach is therefore **not situated on Kolokytha islet**. It lies on the peninsula opposite the islet. Some sources call it Agios Loukas beach; others use only Kolokytha. citeturn21search5turn21search17turn24search13

Access descriptions conflict. A recent local report states approximately 200 metres of walking from the point where vehicles stop, while Lonely Planet gives approximately 500 metres from the parking area below Agios Loukas. Another current guide says there is no dedicated parking. These differences probably reflect different stopping points and changing informal parking behaviour; this is an inference, not a verified explanation. citeturn21search2turn21search5turn21search16

The safe article wording is:

> “The approach includes an unpaved track and a final footpath. There is no dependable formal beach car park, and the walking distance varies according to where vehicles can safely stop.”

Avoid promising that ordinary rental cars can be driven to a reliable parking area beside the path. Road surface, rental-car restrictions, congestion and parking control are all volatile. citeturn21search2turn21search5turn24search19

Kolokytha receives excursion-boat swimmers in summer. Sources agree that the small cove can become busy when boats arrive, although exact arrival windows vary and should not be hard-coded without checking current operators. The absence of toilets, showers, lifeguard and dependable food service is better established than any claim of tranquillity. citeturn21search5turn21search12turn21search16turn24search13

The peninsula also contains Vathi/Armos and remote northern coves including Oxo Harakia, Skourgeto and Pelekiti. These are legitimate geographic bathing places, but they should appear in a secondary section titled “Remote coves for self-sufficient visitors,” not beside Schisma and Plaka as equivalent conventional beaches. Access descriptions are insufficiently authoritative to recommend detailed overland routes. citeturn24search7turn24search9

**Plaka and the Elounda boundary**

Plaka lies opposite Spinalonga on the western shore of the Elounda lagoon. The Municipality describes it as a fishing village with a fishing shelter and pebble beaches. Geographic sources identify it as part of the community of Vrouchas in the Municipality of Agios Nikolaos. citeturn15view1turn24search20

Calling Plaka “an Elounda beach” is acceptable only as a broad visitor-area description. Recommended wording:

> “Plaka, five kilometres north of central Elounda, has pebble swimming areas along the wider Elounda lagoon shoreline.”

Avoid:

> “Plaka Beach is in Elounda town.”

The regional tourism authority describes Plaka as a pebble beach that is partly organised, with umbrellas, loungers, watersports and some tamarisk shade. Operational details remain seasonal. The harbour and Spinalonga boat departures mean that swimmers should distinguish the designated beach sectors from the immediate boat-launch and mooring areas. citeturn14search0turn15view1

Current summer public transport makes Plaka practical without a car. The KTEL schedule valid from 1 July 2026 listed departures from Agios Nikolaos to Plaka at two-hour intervals from 09:00 to 19:00, with corresponding returns; because the schedule changes substantially in winter, the future article should link to KTEL rather than print times. citeturn22search5turn22search13

The 2026 Blue Flag name “Plaka/Phaea Blue” may refer to a resort-managed sector south of the village rather than the entirety of the public village waterfront. The national monitoring profile “Plaka” and the commercial award name cannot be matched with full certainty from the available crawl. This should remain a research caveat. citeturn15view0turn16search1

**Driros and Tsifliki**

Driros is a recognised, hotel-associated beach south of Plaka and was listed as Driros/Domes of Elounda in the 2026 Blue Flag announcement. Travel material describes a pebbly, serviced shore, but access for non-guests and exact current facilities require direct confirmation with the operator or Municipality. citeturn15view0turn24search15

Tsifliki is less clearly bounded. Mapping and beach directories use the name for small shore sections between Elounda and Plaka. The coast also contains unnamed roadside pebble coves. It is better to discuss these collectively than invent individual official beach names from map pins. Parking consists mainly of limited roadside spaces or pull-offs; that detail should be field checked for legality and safety. citeturn24search4turn24search6turn24search12

**Southern Elounda resort coast**

The national registry profile places Poros approximately nine kilometres north of Agios Nikolaos and three kilometres south of Elounda. It describes a 500-metre enclosed bay, a rocky seabed, imported fine material, substantial concrete modification, shallow water and a gentle slope, with five-metre depth reached at an estimated 180 metres from shore. citeturn19search0

The same profile states that access was through two hotel complexes and recorded toilets, showers, umbrellas, loungers, watersports, a lifeguard and mobility-access structures. Those observations come from a profile issued in May 2015. Physical shape and seabed are relatively durable; commercial access, lifeguarding and equipment are not. citeturn19search0

Poros North is separately registered. Its profile also associates it with an organised, modified hotel cove, but the available source is older and its physical description partially repeats the Poros text. Pigadakia Elounda is another official monitored point on the southern coast. In 2026 the Municipality listed Pigadakia Elounda/Elounda Village among its Blue Flag beaches. citeturn15view0turn16search1

These southern beaches belong in the dossier because they are geographically and commercially identified with Elounda. In the future article, however, they need a separate label:

> “Serviced resort beaches south of Elounda—confirm access if you are not staying at the adjoining hotel.”

## Water quality, environment, safety and accessibility

**Official monitoring framework**

Greece’s bathing-water programme implements Directive 2006/7/EC and monitors microbiological indicators, principally intestinal enterococci and *Escherichia coli*. The national registry publishes site profiles, seasonal samples and classifications. The European Environment Agency’s 2025 assessment is based on the four bathing seasons from 2022 through 2025; it should not be interpreted as a real-time reading for every unmonitored cove. citeturn4search4turn12search1

The Directive’s classification addresses faecal-indicator bacteria. It does not by itself establish that water is free of every chemical pollutant, litter problem, algal event, jellyfish occurrence or localised short-term incident. citeturn12search1turn18search12

The Municipality of Agios Nikolaos announced 27 Blue Flag beaches for 2026. Blue Flag requires “Excellent” bathing-water quality and compliance with additional criteria involving cleanliness, information, environmental management and swimmer safety. The award lasts one year and is not a substitute for checking current on-site conditions. citeturn15view0

**Environmental considerations**

Kolokytha and the less-developed peninsula coves should be framed as low-infrastructure natural coast, not as empty land available for unrestricted vehicle parking, fires, large picnics or waste disposal. Official Natura material explains that protected-area designations seek the long-term conservation of important habitats and species, but the research did not obtain an authoritative parcel-level statement proving that every Kolokytha bathing cove lies within a specific Natura polygon. That exact boundary requires GIS verification through the official INSPIRE or Natura viewer before publication. citeturn21search3turn21search6turn21search13

Claims about extensive Posidonia oceanica meadows immediately at each beach were not sufficiently verified. Seaweed or seagrass reported at Chiona should not automatically be identified as Posidonia without botanical or mapped habitat evidence. citeturn24search11

The official Poros profile notes that its enclosed form and shallow water reduce flushing relative to an open coast, while also recording no observed harmful algal or phytoplankton events in the profile evidence. This supports cautious wording about enclosed-bay hydrology, not a claim that Poros has a present pollution problem. citeturn19search0

Boat activity is a practical pressure at Plaka, the Elounda harbour, the Poros channel and Kolokytha. The relevant editorial implication is spatial: recommend swimming in identifiable bathing sectors and away from harbour entrances, moorings and excursion-boat approaches. The national profiles themselves advise against swimming inside ports and permanent mooring areas. citeturn19search0turn21search5turn15view1

**Seasonal notes**

July and August create the greatest pressure on parking, sunbed availability, small coves and boat-swim stops. Exact crowd rankings cannot be verified objectively, but multiple independent sources agree that Kolokytha becomes busy when excursion boats arrive. Plaka’s road and parking also serve Spinalonga departures, tavernas and beach visitors simultaneously. citeturn21search5turn21search12turn24search13turn15view1

Public transport changes materially between summer and winter. The February 2026 schedule provided only limited Plaka services, while the July schedule offered regular daily services. Any final article should say “seasonal KTEL service” and link to the current timetable. citeturn22search5turn22search13

Sunbeds, lifeguards, toilets, showers, water activities, hotel entry procedures and seasonal cafés should carry a visible “checked [month/year]” note if mentioned. Current Blue Flag status supports a general level of management at awarded sectors but does not establish the operating hours of each facility on a particular day. citeturn15view0

**Final research conclusions**

- **Strongest article angle:** a map-led practical explanation of distinct swimming environments, not a ranked “best beaches” list.
- **Precise geographic scope:** Pigadakia and Poros in the south; Schisma, Chiona and the Poros channel in central Elounda; accessible Kolokytha peninsula coves; Tsifliki and Driros; and Plaka in the Vrouchas community.
- **Verified core beach list:** Schisma, Chiona, Kolokytha, Plaka, Driros, Poros, Poros North and Pigadakia.
- **Recognised secondary swimming places:** Poros channel edge, Vathi/Armos, northern Kolokytha coves and Tsifliki roadside coves.
- **Primary unresolved issues:** exact current facilities, hotel access, Kolokytha parking and road conditions, 2026 sample dates, monitoring-point boundaries and minor-cove naming.
- **Highest-value next step:** a structured field survey accompanied by municipal and bathing-water-registry confirmation, rather than additional collection of generic travel descriptions.
