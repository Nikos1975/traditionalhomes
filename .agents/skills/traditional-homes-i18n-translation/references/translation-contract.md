# Translation Contract

## English master

English is the verified factual and substantive master for localized pages.

A translation must preserve:

- factual statements;
- numbers, dates, distances, capacities, prices, hours and conditions;
- qualifications and uncertainty;
- cautions and practical limitations;
- section-level information coverage.

A translation may change:

- sentence structure;
- idiom and register;
- generic UI terminology;
- title/meta/H1 wording for natural search-language expression;
- localized generic URL segments when infrastructure already supports them.

A translation must not:

- add unsupported facts or claims;
- remove inconvenient substantive information;
- introduce luxury/sales language;
- silently correct a suspected English error in one locale;
- duplicate structured property facts into a second locale inventory.

## Cross-language correction rule

If the English source appears wrong or outdated, create a proposed cross-language correction containing:

- exact source path/claim;
- why it may be wrong;
- evidence needed or available;
- affected locales/routes;
- current action: leave aligned / approved correction.

Until approved, keep locales aligned to the verified English source.
